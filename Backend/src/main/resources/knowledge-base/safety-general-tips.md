# Personal Safety — Everyday Guidance

General, widely-recommended personal safety practices. These are habits
and awareness tips, not a substitute for professional security or legal
advice.

## Trust your instincts

If a situation, person, or place feels wrong, treat that feeling as real
information, not an overreaction. It's okay to leave, change your route,
make an excuse, or ask for help even if you can't fully explain why
something feels off. UnSocial's Fake Call and Fake Message features exist
specifically to give you a low-friction way to exit a situation without
needing to justify it to anyone.

## Walking alone

Stick to well-lit, populated routes where possible, especially at night.
Walk facing oncoming traffic on roads without proper footpaths so you can
see vehicles approaching. Avoid being absorbed in your phone or wearing
both headphones at full volume — staying aware of your surroundings is
one of the most effective deterrents. Share your route or start a Live
Tracking session with someone you trust before heading out, particularly
on an unfamiliar path.

## Public transport and rideshares

Share your trip details (driver name, vehicle number, route) with someone
before getting in, and verify the vehicle and driver match what the app
shows before entering. Sit near the driver or in a populated carriage on
public transport when possible. If a ride feels unsafe at any point, ask
the driver to stop in a public, well-lit area rather than waiting until
the destination. A Live Tracking link shared with a trusted contact for
the duration of the ride adds a layer of accountability.

## Going out and social situations

Keep your phone charged — carry a power bank if you'll be out for a long
stretch. Watch your own drink being made or poured where possible, and
don't leave drinks unattended. Agree on a check-in plan with a friend
beforehand, or set an UnSocial Safety Timer so someone is automatically
notified if you don't check in. If you ever need to leave a date,
gathering, or conversation without an awkward confrontation, Fake Call and
Fake Message are built for exactly that.

## At home

Don't announce when you're home alone, especially on social media or to
unfamiliar callers/visitors at the door. Verify the identity of delivery
or service personnel before opening the door fully. Keep entry points
locked even when you're inside, and know your nearest neighbors well
enough to call on them in an emergency.

## Digital safety

Be cautious about sharing real-time location publicly on social media —
posting "live" from a specific place lets anyone watching know exactly
where you are right now. Review which apps have access to your location
and only grant it where genuinely needed. If you suspect someone has
installed tracking software (stalkerware) on your device without your
consent, that's a serious privacy violation — consider getting a trusted
person or a professional to check the device, and document what you find
before removing anything, since the evidence itself may matter if you
decide to report it.
